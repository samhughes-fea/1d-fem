## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Brazier flattening in corotational GBT beams** ([element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md](element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md#brazier-flattening)). `CorotationalGBTBeamElement3D` adds the axial strain of the fibres that the modes move in the section plane, `e_B = sum_k a_k g_k . kappa`, so a bent section's flanges close in and its bending stiffness falls. `SectionAnalysis.gbt_brazier_terms` builds the terms on thin-walled and 2D sections. An isotropic tube ovalises as Brazier's closed form within 1 %. On the CAS composite box of [nonlinear_solid_box](../post_processing/validation_visualisers/nonlinear_solid_box/README.md), the beam with eight modes flattens 86 % as much as the `C3D20R` solid at P and follows its tip within 0.3 % up to 2 P (Timoshenko: 1.3 %). At 3 P (49 % of the span) its tip is 3.5 % stiff, against 5.3 % for the rigid-section Timoshenko beam. The remaining gap is the solid's flattening growing faster than the square of the curvature beyond 2 P.
- **Layup sensitivities of Benscoter beams and of buckling loads** ([element_library/layup_sensitivity.md](element_library/layup_sensitivity.md)). `section_derivative` now differentiates the Benscoter 8 x 8 stiffness and warping inertia (through the torsional warping solve and its shear-centre shift) and the second-order terms, exactly. Static and eigen jobs with `LinearBenscoterBeamElement3D` write `layup_sensitivity.csv`, and linear buckling jobs with Timoshenko elements take a `buckling` response: the load factor's derivative from the buckling mode, with the geometric stiffness differentiated through the section and, by one adjoint solve, through the prestress. Pretwisted, curved and tapered elements are now included: the curved element's condensation and the pretwist coupling are differentiated, and `section_taper.txt` gains an optional `[Section_Taper_Laminate]` block (written by `blade_beam_job`) that splits the Resal coupling by laminate. All agree with central differences of re-solved jobs to 2e-5. Sections meshed in 2D take ply-angle sensitivities too (`solid.layup_sensitivity.solid_section_derivative`): `SectionMesh.from_thin_walled` tags each element with its laminate ply, kept in `section_mesh_2d.txt` by a new optional `[Section_Element_Ply]` block, and the material rotation, the Giavotto solves, the Benscoter terms and the second-order terms are differentiated exactly. GBT beams (`LinearGBTBeamElement3D`, one thin-walled section) take them as well: `gbt_section_derivative` differentiates the GBT stiffness, mass and geometric tensors together with the distortional, Saint-Venant warping and shear-lag modes, from their eigenproblems (holding the modes fixed puts a distortional frequency's thickness derivative 18 % off). Static, eigen and buckling GBT jobs match central differences to 3e-6 (2e-5 with shear-lag modes).
- **Blade torsion frequencies on the tapered, pretwisted GBT beam, and where the remaining gap comes from** ([examples/blade_beam](../examples/blade_beam/README.md#modal)). The example's GBT beam on 2D sections is now tapered and pretwisted like the shell. Its first torsion frequency is 4.0 % below the shell's, converged in modes and elements. `sandwich_strip_check.py` shows why: the shell reference's composite `S8R` shells, expanded by CalculiX into one brick through the wall, give the sandwich skin no core shear (2.3 % high against layered bricks and sandwich theory over a 1 m span, 8.9 % over 0.5 m). With the foam made stiff in transverse shear as in the shell, the GBT beam is within 0.3 % of the shell in the first torsion mode and 2.8 % in the second.
- **GBT on tapered and pretwisted beams** ([element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md](element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md#tapered-and-pretwisted-beams)). `LinearGBTBeamElement3D` shares its warping and mode amplitudes between elements, so on a beam with a section per element each section's bases must continue those of its neighbour. `gbt_alignment.align_gbt_sections` turns each section's warping and distortional bases to its neighbour's by orthogonal Procrustes on the fields' shape moments (`SectionAnalysis.gbt_moments`), which undoes rotations within a block, swaps and sign flips while leaving each section's model unchanged, and warns where two sections' bases do not continue each other. Thin-walled and 2D section jobs align their GBT sections, and `with_taper` and `with_pretwist` now add their couplings to `gbt_stiffness`, so `blade_beam_job` takes GBT with `taper` and `pretwist`. On the IEA 15 MW outboard segment, GBT with the modes held gives the Timoshenko 2D tip deflections within 0.3 % in flap and edge; in torsion it is 3.7 % stiffer from the root's warping restraint.
- **Interlaminar stresses checked against a 3D solid** ([examples/blade_section_2d](../examples/blade_section_2d/README.md#against-a-3d-solid)). The 70 % span IEA section's 2D mesh, extruded into a CalculiX prism of `C3D20` bricks (`make_interlaminar_reference.py`, committed reference), gives the interface stresses of a mid-length slice under the example's section forces. On 2472 interface points the largest interlaminar shear is at the same pressure-side cap-to-skin interface in both, 2.75 MPa in the 2D section against 3.00 MPa (8 % low), and the RMS difference is 0.8 % of the largest τxn. `prism.write_deck` takes `element="C3D20"`: reduced-integration bricks give interlaminar shear that alternates in sign between thin ply elements.
- **API reference** ([api/README.md](api/README.md)), generated from the modules' docstrings and `__all__` by `python scripts/build_api_reference.py`: sections, elements (every `element_type` a job accepts), job input, solvers, post-processing and `process_job`. `tests/test_api_reference.py` checks every listed module imports and the committed pages list the same modules. The README now describes what 1d-fem does, how to install it and run a job, the job files and the examples.
- **Design sensitivities to ply angles and thicknesses** ([element_library/layup_sensitivity.md](element_library/layup_sensitivity.md), [examples/thin_walled/layup_sensitivity](../examples/thin_walled/layup_sensitivity/README.md)). With `layup_sensitivity.txt` (ply parameters and responses), a static or eigen job writes `layup_sensitivity.csv`: the derivatives of nodal displacements, the compliance and natural frequencies with respect to chosen ply angles and thicknesses. The thin-walled section's stiffness and mass are differentiated exactly (laminate, wall-law condensation and the section's warping solutions; `thin_walled.layup_sensitivity.section_derivative`), and the job derivatives follow by the adjoint method and the eigenvalue derivative, element by element, from one solve. On a bend-twist coupled box spar all 63 derivatives agree with central differences of re-solved jobs to 1e-5, and a Newton loop on the off-axis ply angle reaches a target twist per flap deflection in four jobs. Linear static and eigen jobs with Timoshenko elements on thin-walled sections.
- **Local, distortional and global buckling with GBT elements** ([element_library/gbt_buckling.md](element_library/gbt_buckling.md), [examples/thin_walled/gbt_buckling](../examples/thin_walled/gbt_buckling/README.md)). `LinearGBTBeamElement3D` now has a geometric stiffness. It takes the prebuckling membrane stresses of its own GBT strains and works them against the quadratic strains of the in-plane motion of the section, rigid and modal (`CrossSectionModes.geometric_tensor`, and `SolidCrossSectionModes.geometric_tensor` for 2D sections). Linear buckling and P-delta jobs then find local plate buckling, distortional buckling and global buckling in one model. A square tube buckles locally within 0.03 % of the plate value, and a lipped channel column follows a finite strip model of full plate theory within 1.3 % from 40 mm to 3 m. Buckling problems of more than 600 free DOFs are solved by sparse Lanczos.
- **Ply failure criteria with margins at the Gauss points** ([element_library/ply_failure.md](element_library/ply_failure.md), [examples/thin_walled/ply_failure](../examples/thin_walled/ply_failure/README.md)). `pre_processing/section_integration/ply_failure.py` gives maximum stress, Tsai-Wu, Hashin (3D) and Puck exposures, which scale with the load, so `1 / f` is the reserve factor. Puck's inter-fibre failure is found on the most stressed action plane by a search over its angle; in plane stress it reproduces modes A, B and C. With `ply_strength.txt` (strengths per material id), a static job writes `ply_failure.csv` at every Gauss point of every element, from the cached element objects. It covers every ply face of thin-walled sections and every quadrature point of 2D sections, with a per-element summary. On a CFRP box spar the thin-walled and 2D sections agree within 2 % on first-ply failure (Hashin, Puck, maximum stress).
- **Large-deflection check against a CalculiX solid** ([nonlinear_solid_box](../post_processing/validation_visualisers/nonlinear_solid_box/README.md)). The CAS composite box, extruded into `C3D20R` bricks and run in `NLGEOM` up to three times the force for a linear 20 % span deflection. The corotational Timoshenko beam follows it within 1.3 % in tip deflection and 2 % in twist up to 35 % of the span. At 49 % it is 5 % stiff, because the solid's flanges flatten (Brazier, 3.3 % of the depth), which a rigid-section beam cannot show. The prism deck (`iea15mw_sections/prism.py`) now takes custom steps, and at odd brick levels its junction ties use the brick face's interpolation.
- **GBT on 2D sections, and corotational GBT, Reddy and Levinson beams** ([element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md](element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md#gbt-on-2d-sections), [element_library/corotational_section_beams.md](element_library/corotational_section_beams.md)). `LinearGBTBeamElement3D` takes sections meshed in 2D with `[modes]`: `SolidCrossSectionModes` builds the distortional modes from the in-plane eigenmodes of the meshed walls, with their axial warping from the shear-energy minimiser, and integrates the 3D material law over the mesh. On a thin aluminium box it gives the thin-walled GBT statics within 3 %, and with four modes the lowest eight frequencies of a CalculiX `C3D20R` box within 5 % (flap within 1 %), except the torsion-distortion mode, which is 8 % high. `CorotationalGBTBeamElement3D`, `CorotationalReddyBeamElement3D` and `CorotationalLevinsonBeamElement3D` run the GBT and third-order beams at large displacement; at small load they are their linear elements, and under a large tip moment or load they follow the corotational Timoshenko beam.
- **Follower loads** ([examples/follower_loads](../examples/follower_loads/README.md), [conventions/JOB_INPUT_FOLLOWER_LOADS.md](conventions/JOB_INPUT_FOLLOWER_LOADS.md)). `follower_load.txt` puts nodal forces and moments on a nonlinear static job that turn with the node's rotation (`R F_0` on the translations, `J^T R M_0` on the rotation vector), and every Newton iteration adds their unsymmetric load stiffness (`processing/static/follower_loads.py`). Other job types refuse the file. A cantilever of 40 corotational Timoshenko elements under a tip force that stays normal to the tip matches the follower elastica within 1e-3 L up to P L²/EI = 6, and the same beam under a dead force matches the dead-load elastica within 2.4e-4 L.
- **Rotating beams** ([examples/rotating_beam](../examples/rotating_beam/README.md), [conventions/SIMULATION_SETTINGS_TAXONOMY.md](conventions/SIMULATION_SETTINGS_TAXONOMY.md)). A `[Rotation]` block (`omega` or `rpm`, `axis`, `origin`) analyses the structure in a frame spinning at a constant rate. Each element forms its centrifugal load, spin softening, rotation-load stiffness (the propeller moment) and Coriolis matrix in its own Gauss loop, from four point masses per station that carry the section's mass properties and move with the element's field interpolation (`pre_processing/element_library/rotating_frame.py`). Static jobs add the centrifugal load and softening. Eigen jobs add the centrifugal stiffening and the Coriolis terms, solved as a first-order system, and `omega_sweep` writes a Campbell table. Checked against Hodges and Rutkowski's rotating cantilever (flap within 0.014 %), the exact lag and whirl relations, the propeller moment and the centrifugal stretch of a bar.
- **Taper coupling for tapered beams** ([conventions/JOB_INPUT_THIN_WALLED_SECTIONS.md](conventions/JOB_INPUT_THIN_WALLED_SECTIONS.md)). A wall inclined to the beam axis carries part of the shear in its axial stress (the Resal effect), so a prismatic element over a tapered blade is too soft in shear. `section_taper` computes the coupling of the section strains with the transverse shear strains from the wall slopes between two cuts (`C = int A f r'^T ds`, `Q = int A r' r'^T ds`, helical fibres of a turning section included), `SectionAnalysis.with_taper` adds it to the element stiffness, and `section_taper.txt` assigns it per element. `blade_beam_job` writes it by default (`taper=True`). On the IEA 15 MW outboard segment the flap tip deflection goes from 5.5 % soft to within 0.5 % of the shell, and the first flap frequency from 3.3 % to 0.3 % low.
- **Plots drawn from Gauss-point data and shape functions.** `post_processing/solid/gauss_projection.py` projects each `Q8` section element's nine Gauss values over the element with Lagrange shapes in the Gauss points' own coordinates, and `post_processing/graphical_visualisers/beam_field_sampling.py` samples displacement fields along a beam with each element's own interpolation. The blade, 2D section, box spar and three-cell examples now draw continuous curves only through these, per element or per ply, and show discrete results (load steps, increments, contour points) as markers.
- **Blade examples from implicit-geometry-kernel** ([examples/blade_beam](../examples/blade_beam/README.md), [examples/blade_section_2d](../examples/blade_section_2d/README.md)). `blade_beam` lofts the outboard 20 m of the IEA 15 MW blade, builds Timoshenko and Benscoter beams on thin-walled and 2D sections, and compares static tip response, frequencies and a large flapwise deflection with a composite-laminate-model / CalculiX shell blade. Edge deflection is within 1 %, and the first flap and edge frequencies are within 3.3 %. Flap deflection is within 0.5 % with the taper coupling (5.5 % soft without it). Torsion is 1.5 % stiff with Benscoter elements and 4 % with Timoshenko elements, and the torsion frequencies are 7 to 10 % high. `blade_section_2d` shows the 2D section stresses at 70 % span: cap stresses against the thin-walled model, interlaminar shear on every ply interface (largest at the cap and web junction), and through-thickness stresses in a sandwich panel.
- **IEA 15 MW section validation** ([post_processing/validation_visualisers/iea15mw_sections](../post_processing/validation_visualisers/iea15mw_sections/README.md)). The thin-walled and 2D sections of three IEA Wind 15 MW stations (root, max chord, 70 % span), cut by implicit-geometry-kernel with one idealised layup, are compared with a composite-laminate-model shell segment, PreComp and a CalculiX `C3D20R` prism (6×6 from the strain energies of 21 load cases). Thin-walled and shell agree within 0.14 % in EA and EI, 0.43 % in GJ and 0.11 % of chord in the centres. At the root the 2D section equals the prism within 0.01 %. Outboard the 2D caps hold 6.5 % more carbon than the thin-walled caps; with the carbon matched, EA and EI agree within 1.3 %. The station data and a 20 m tapered segment are in `examples/iea15mw`. Thin-walled sections with 400 or more unknowns now use a sparse LU, about 4 times faster on blade cuts.
- **Stress output at stations, for corotational and modal runs, and on ply interfaces** ([element_library/solid_section_analysis.md](element_library/solid_section_analysis.md)). `stress_stations.txt` picks where `wall_stresses.csv` and `section_stresses.csv` are written (planes `X = x` or nearest axis points) instead of element mid-points. Nonlinear jobs of corotational elements write the stresses of each element's local deformation, and eigen jobs write one block per mode (`mode` column). `SolidSectionAnalysis.interface_stresses` recovers the interlaminar stresses on every ply interface by a per-ply fit through the two rows nearest it, written to `interface_stresses.csv`. It is exact for a cross-ply strip with two rows per ply, where the raw element edge values are up to 15 % off. Both stress files gain `xi` and `mode` columns, and their `x` is now the global `X` of the axis point, which was the local coordinate on rotated elements.
- **Corotational beams on rotated, kinked and pretwisted geometry** ([element_library/element_frames.md](element_library/element_frames.md)). `CorotationalEulerBernoulliBeamElement3D`, `CorotationalTimoshenkoBeamElement3D` and `CorotationalBenscoterBeamElement3D` take element frames and `[twist]`, start from their undeformed frame, and take the pretwist rate from `precurvature.txt`; a prebent or curved axis is given by its nodes. `blade_beam_job` accepts them and then writes nonlinear static settings (`NONLINEAR_SETTINGS`). Checked by a turned cantilever at large deflection (equal to 1e-8 L), a quarter circle closing into a semicircle under a tip moment (exact solution, within 0.2 %), a prebent cantilever tending to the elastica, and a prebent pretwisted blade through the nonlinear runner.
- **Rotated, curved and pretwisted GBT, Reddy and Levinson beams** ([element_library/element_frames.md](element_library/element_frames.md)). `LinearGBTBeamElement3D` and the third-order beams now take element frames, `[twist]` and `precurvature.txt` geometry like the Euler-Bernoulli, Timoshenko and Benscoter beams. GBT mode amplitudes map to themselves; the third-order slope DOFs are carried at nodes as shear strains, so slopes stay continuous at kinks. Checked by turned cantilevers (equal to round-off) and a quarter-circle cantilever (Reddy and Levinson within 0.06 % of Roark; a curved GBT box equal to its chain of straight elements).
- **Distributed and wall loads on rotated, curved and pretwisted beams** ([element_library/element_frames.md](element_library/element_frames.md)). `distributed_load.txt` loads (global components per length of the axis, sampled along global `X`) are spread along each element's own geometry with its work-conjugate fields, sub-element by sub-element on curved ones. Wall loads are built in each element's (or sub-element's) own axes. Both were refused on such meshes before. `blade_beam_job` takes `line_loads`. Checked by turned cantilevers (equal to round-off), a quarter-circle cantilever under line and wall loads (4 curved elements within 0.3 % of 64 straight ones) and a blade under a flapwise line load.
- **Blade beam jobs from implicit-geometry-kernel** ([conventions/JOB_INPUT_THIN_WALLED_SECTIONS.md](conventions/JOB_INPUT_THIN_WALLED_SECTIONS.md)). `blade_beam_job` writes a cantilever job for a `BladeGeometry`: nodes on the pitch axis at the station offsets (sweep, prebend), one section per element cut at its mid-span (thin-walled, or a layered 2D mesh with `kind="2d"`), and the twist gradient as each element's pretwist rate in `precurvature.txt`. `format_laminate_file` and `format_section_geometry` write `laminate.txt` and `section_geometry.txt`.
- **2D section meshes of implicit-geometry-kernel blade sections** ([element_library/solid_section_mesh.md](element_library/solid_section_mesh.md)). `section_mesh_from_cut` builds conforming layered `Q8` meshes that follow the true wall geometry: skins grow inward from the outer mould line, caps sit inside the skin, and webs land on the innermost face with shared nodes. There is one or more element rows per ply, with no overlaps or ties, and the trailing edge is closed. `write_section_mesh_2d` writes `section_mesh_2d.txt`. Results are within 0.5 % of the thin-walled section for isotropic 0A and 2A-F sections, within 1.1 % for a composite 2D-F box spar, and converged to 0.2 % under refinement.
- **Thin-walled sections from implicit-geometry-kernel** ([conventions/JOB_INPUT_THIN_WALLED_SECTIONS.md](conventions/JOB_INPUT_THIN_WALLED_SECTIONS.md)). `thin_walled_from_cut` turns a `SectionCut` of an implicit-geometry-kernel blade into a `ThinWalledSection`: one wall per mid-surface segment, spar caps stacked inside their skins, laminates bound by region label, and section coordinates measured from the pitch axis. `region_laminate.txt` binds labels to `laminate.txt` ids. On a 2D-F NACA 0024 section it matches anisotropic-section-model's `from_geometry` within 0.5 % in EA, edgewise EI and GJ, and in shear centre within 0.1 % of chord. The flapwise EI is 2.1 % lower, because 1d-fem keeps the cap-on-skin coupling `B`. The geometry kernel is optional (Python 3.11 or later).

- **3D stresses and restrained warping on 2D sections** ([element_library/solid_section_analysis.md](element_library/solid_section_analysis.md)). `SolidSectionAnalysis.point_stresses` and `stress_at` give the 3D stresses over the section in section, material (ply or core) and wall axes, including interlaminar stresses. Element recovery covers Euler-Bernoulli, Timoshenko and Benscoter elements, and static jobs write `section_stresses.csv`. `benscoter_stiffness` (the 7th warping DOF, with in-plane relaxation of the warping gradient) lets `LinearBenscoterBeamElement3D` use 2D sections, and `warping_constant` is now its `K[7, 7]`. Checked against layered-beam interlaminar shear, sandwich core shear, Vlasov's `E Γ` and the thin-walled Benscoter section.
- **2D section solver** ([element_library/solid_section_analysis.md](element_library/solid_section_analysis.md)): `SolidSectionAnalysis` solves the Giavotto (BECAS) warping problem on a 2D section mesh with 3D anisotropic materials. It gives the coupled 6 × 6 stiffness, the shear and tension centres, `E Γ`, mass and second-order terms of any section shape, and `SectionAnalysis.from_solid` passes them to the beam elements. Jobs assign them with `section_mesh_2d.txt` and `element_section.txt`. Exact for homogeneous anisotropic sections under axial force and bending; within 0.2 % of the thin-walled analysis (`hoop="free"`) for the extension, bending and torsion stiffness of a CFRP I-section, and within 0.8 % for the whole 6 × 6 of a CAS box.
- **2D section meshes and 3D materials** ([element_library/solid_section_mesh.md](element_library/solid_section_mesh.md)): `T6` and `Q8` section elements; orthotropic `SolidMaterial` rotated to its ply angle and wall direction (it reduces to the thin-walled ply stiffness under plane stress); meshes from a rectangle, from a thin-walled section (a strip per wall, a row per ply, tied junctions), from Gmsh 4.1 files and from `section_mesh_2d.txt`. The input of the 2D section solver.
- **Beams in any direction, section twist, curved and pretwisted beams** ([element_library/element_frames.md](element_library/element_frames.md)). The linear Euler-Bernoulli, Timoshenko and Benscoter beams take their axis from the node coordinates and an optional `[twist]` column in `element.txt` (degrees about the axis), and their stiffness, mass, loads, geometric stiffness and wall stresses are rotated to global axes. `precurvature.txt` now makes an element the stress-free arc of that curvature and twist between its nodes, built from condensed straight sub-elements, and a twist rate adds the helical-fibre extension-twist coupling to the section (`SectionAnalysis.with_pretwist`). Checked against a turned cantilever, a quarter-circle cantilever, a helical spring and a pretwisted strip.
- **Breaking:** for those three element types `precurvature.txt` no longer adds the equivalent load `Bᵀ D E₀` on a straight element; the curvature is in the geometry. On meshes with rotated or curved elements, point loads must sit on nodes (global components) and distributed and wall loads are refused. Other element types keep the old meaning of `precurvature.txt`.

- **Shell validation of the second-order and corotational beams** ([post_processing/validation_visualisers/abaqus/shell_validation](../post_processing/validation_visualisers/abaqus/shell_validation/README.md)). Five cases written as Abaqus S4R decks from the same section description the beam uses: channel flexural-torsional buckling, I-beam lateral-torsional buckling, the I-beam's prebuckling path near that moment (NLGEOM), large twist of an I-beam with restrained warping (NLGEOM) and a bend-twist coupled CFRP box under large tip deflection (NLGEOM). `run.py` writes the decks, runs Abaqus, extracts the ODB (`extract_odb.py`), reduces each station to beam displacements by a rigid section fit and compares with cached 1d-fem results against a 3 % target. The same decks run in CalculiX (`--solver calculix`, S8R), which checks the buckling cases and, linearly, the nonlinear decks.

### Changed

- **CI takes about half the time.** Tests run in parallel (`pytest -n auto`, `pytest-xdist` added to `requirements-ci.txt`): on GitHub, Python 3.10 takes 17 minutes instead of 32 and Python 3.11 (with the geometry kernel tests) 30 minutes instead of 41 to 53. Each commit is tested once (pull requests, and pushes to `main`) instead of once per push and once per pull request, a newer push cancels the run it supersedes, and pip downloads are cached. `--durations=20` lists the slowest tests.

### Fixed

- **2D-section local buckling was 10 % high** ([element_library/gbt_buckling.md](element_library/gbt_buckling.md#checks)). The axial strain of each 2D distortional mode's `a''` held the wall in plane strain, so its longitudinal plate bending stiffness `C` was 22 % high (\(\nu = 0.3\)). `SolidCrossSectionModes` now adds a slaved strain across the wall that makes \(\sigma_{nn} = 0\), as in plate theory, and `C` equals the thin-walled value to 0.03 %. The 2D local buckling stress of the 300 × 120 × 2 mm box converges on the thin-walled GBT and finite strip value from above, +0.9 % at 48 elements along each wall (it was +10 %). Against the CalculiX box, the torsion-distortion frequency of the 2D GBT beam is 4 % high instead of 8 %.

- **Linear static diagnostics on large systems.** The dense SVD for the rank and condition number now runs up to 1500 equations (and once instead of twice); a 3500-DOF job spent minutes in it.
- **GBT element energy with coupled mode amplitudes** ([element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md](element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md)). `LinearGBTBeamElement3D` integrated its shear block (\(\gamma\), \(b\)) on one point and the rest of the section stiffness, including the block's coupling with the cubic mode amplitudes, on the full rule. With coupled sections the element energy could go negative: GBT on the IEA blade's 2D sections had a spurious 5.2 Hz distortional mode, and with 8 modes the eigen solve failed. The shear block is now an assumed (B-bar) strain, the centre value by default, and the whole energy is on the full rule. Static results of uncoupled sections are unchanged.
- **Eigen solve with massless DOFs.** `smallest_generalized_eigenpairs` now starts with shift-invert just below zero, and falls back to `which="SM"`. `SM` needs a positive definite mass, and GBT warping amplitudes carry none, so its lowest modes could be wrong without an error.
- **Blade torsion frequencies with distortion inertia** ([examples/blade_beam](../examples/blade_beam/README.md#modal)). The example adds GBT on 2D sections with four distortional modes. Their inertia takes the first torsion frequency from 10 % high to 4 % low against the shell, on the same untapered beam. `blade_beam_job` takes `LinearGBTBeamElement3D` with `gbt_modes`, and `prescribed_displacement.txt` takes `Q*` for all the extra DOFs of a node.
- **No pinned junction ties in 2D thin-walled section meshes.** `SectionMesh.from_thin_walled` now mitres every two-wall corner it can, in open sections as well as closed ones. A corner it cannot mitre (different thicknesses, or walls running against each other) becomes a butt joint: the first wall runs on past it by half the other's thickness, so the other wall's tied end edge lies inside it. T-junctions were already embedded. No tied node is pinned to a strip end any more. In 3 m CalculiX prisms the tip rotations come within 0.95–1.01 of the free-warping beam: an L section was 25 times too stiff in torsion with the old ties, and a two-cell box 1.3 times in bending. `junctions="tie"` keeps the old meshing.
- **Blade torsion findings corrected and narrowed** ([examples/blade_beam](../examples/blade_beam/README.md)). The thin-walled beams twist more than the shell, so they are 4 % (Timoshenko) and 1.5 % (Benscoter) soft in torsion, not stiff as the README said. Cross-section distortion, measured with GBT on the 2D sections, softens the restrained blade by about 1.5 %. The 7–10 % high torsion frequencies are an inertia difference, not a stiffness one: a Timoshenko beam on 2D sections matches the shell's static twist within 0.4 % but its torsion frequency is still 10 % high.
- **Explained: a GBT box with its modes held was 2.2 % stiffer in flap than the Benscoter box.** Holding the flap shear-warping amplitude along the beam keeps the section plane under transverse shear (plane-section shear stiffness). With it free, GBT without distortional modes equals Benscoter in torsion to round-off and is 0.11 % stiffer in flap, from the shear warping restrained at the root ([element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md](element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md)).
- **Explained: PreComp's 6 % higher edgewise EI on the outboard IEA 15 MW sections.** PreComp stacks each lamina inward from the outer surface at full thickness, so near a sharp trailing edge the upper and lower skins overlap and are counted twice, at the largest edgewise lever arm. With the trailing-edge skins clipped to half the local thickness, PreComp's edge EI is within 0.5 % of the thin-walled section at max chord and 70 % span (`make_precomp_reference.py`, `reference/precomp_idealised_te_clipped.json`).
- **2D section mesher at thick trailing edges** (`section_mesh_from_cut`). Where the skins close on each other, each side now fills only up to the medial axis (the points equidistant from both outer faces), and the tip station is no deeper than its neighbours. Before, each side took half of its own ray gap, so the two sides crossed: the IEA 15 MW max-chord section (62 mm skins) inverted an element, and at 70 % span the skins overlapped near the tip, which made the 2D edge EI 0.6 % and GJ 0.6 % too high. The max-chord section now meshes and, with the cap carbon matched, agrees with the thin-walled section within 1.2 %. `examples/blade_section_2d` reports the interface stresses at the sharp trailing-edge tip apart, since they grow with refinement there.
- **Corners of 2D thin-walled section meshes are mitred.** `SectionMesh.from_thin_walled` tied each wall's end edge into the neighbouring wall, pinning the part outside that wall's strip to one node. That made the corners too stiff: distortional GBT modes were missing below 124 Hz on the test box, and a 3D extrusion of the composite box was 1.6 times too stiff in bending and 3 times in torsion. Closed sections with equal ply thicknesses now get mitred, conforming corners (`junctions="auto"`); `junctions="tie"` keeps the old meshing, which open sections still use. The CalculiX box reference of `gbt_2d_box` was rebuilt on the mitred mesh.
- **`section_mesh_2d.txt` kept no junction ties.** 2D sections from `SectionMesh.from_thin_walled` lost their wall-junction ties when written for a job, so their corners became hinges. The file now has a `[Section_Tie]` block. The CalculiX prism deck (`iea15mw_sections/prism.py`) now writes the ties as `*EQUATION` cards as well.
- **`NonlinearTimoshenkoBeamElement3D` bending.** Its internal force and tangent integrated the transverse shear strain on the full Gauss rule, while its `K_0` used the reduced one-point rule. The linear two-node element then locks in `F_int` but not in `K_0`, so Newton converged to a wrong equilibrium: a slender cantilever came out at −16.6 times its exact tip deflection. The shear block now uses the reduced rule throughout. The geometric stiffness is the exact second derivative of the Green–Lagrange strain energy, so the tangent is now the true derivative of `F_int`. `LinearTimoshenkoBeamElement3D` with a warping DOF used the full rule for shear as well and gets the same fix.

### Changed

- **Thin-walled hoop default is now `"free"`** (`M_ss = 0`), the Saint-Venant answer that the 2D section solver matches. It was `"restrained"`. Sections used by GBT elements, and `CrossSectionModes`, still use `"restrained"`, since the cross-section modes carry the hoop bending. Pass `hoop="restrained"` to keep the old stiffness. Unsymmetric and ±45° walls see the largest change, up to 30 % in GJ for a ±45° I-section.
- **Fix: Benscoter torsional warping pole.** `ThinWalledSectionAnalysis` constrained the torsional warping against the `y` and `z` rigid modes while solving it. For a section whose origin is off the shear centre (a channel about its web), this put the pole in the wrong place: `E Γ` came out 16 % low. Only the constant mode is constrained now, and the warping is the same for any origin.

- **Breaking: `LinearReddyBeamElement3D` and `LinearLevinsonBeamElement3D` rewritten** as third-order shear elements derived from the section ([element_library/higher_order_shear.md](element_library/higher_order_shear.md)). The previous elements gave a cantilever tip about 30 times too stiff and did not converge, because their rotation shape functions could not represent a linear rotation.
  - Kinematics: the Timoshenko section motion plus the Saint-Venant shear warping slaved to the shear strain, `eps_xx += Psi(s) . chi` with `chi = gamma' - R psi6`. For a solid rectangle this is Reddy's cubic; for thin walls it is the wall analysis' shear warping.
  - Reddy is variational; Levinson keeps only the classical equilibrium equations (Petrov-Galerkin), which gives Timoshenko deflections for uncoupled sections.
  - Nodes carry two extra DOFs, the slopes `v'` and `w'` (`Q1`, `Q2`), so a mesh with these elements has 8 DOFs per node. Fix `Q1`, `Q2` at a clamp to restrain the shear warping (Reddy).
  - Both elements accept thin-walled sections (`element_section.txt`) and give wall and ply stresses. `[warping] = 1` is refused (use `LinearBenscoterBeamElement3D`), and the `[alpha]` column of `section.txt` is no longer used.
  - `SectionAnalysis` gains `third_order_stiffness` (8 x 8) and `third_order_rate`; `ThinWalledSectionAnalysis` computes them from the walls.
  - Removed: the old `levinson/utilities` and `reddy/utilities` operators, their unit tests and the `scripts/*levinson*` verification scripts.

### Added

- **GBT on 2D sections, and corotational GBT, Reddy and Levinson beams** ([element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md](element_library/gbt_cross_section_modes.md#gbt-on-2d-sections), [element_library/corotational_section_beams.md](element_library/corotational_section_beams.md)). `LinearGBTBeamElement3D` takes sections meshed in 2D with `[modes]`: `SolidCrossSectionModes` builds the distortional modes from the in-plane eigenmodes of the meshed walls, with their axial warping from the shear-energy minimiser, and integrates the 3D material law over the mesh. On a thin aluminium box it gives the thin-walled GBT statics within 3 %, and with four modes the lowest eight frequencies of a CalculiX `C3D20R` box within 5 % (flap within 1 %), except the torsion-distortion mode, which is 8 % high. `CorotationalGBTBeamElement3D`, `CorotationalReddyBeamElement3D` and `CorotationalLevinsonBeamElement3D` run the GBT and third-order beams at large displacement; at small load they are their linear elements, and under a large tip moment or load they follow the corotational Timoshenko beam.
- **Corotational large-displacement beams on anisotropic sections** ([element_library/corotational_section_beams.md](element_library/corotational_section_beams.md)): `CorotationalEulerBernoulliBeamElement3D`, `CorotationalTimoshenkoBeamElement3D` and `CorotationalBenscoterBeamElement3D`.
  - They wrap the linear elements with their coupled `SectionAnalysis` in a corotational frame (Battini and Pacoste). Nodal rotations are total rotation vectors.
  - The local energy adds the cubic second-order term (offsets, Wagner, bimoment) and the quartic large-twist term from `SectionAnalysis.axial_points`.
  - The residual is exact (complex-step `dp/dU`).
  - Checked against the elastica, the semicircle roll-up, Wagner torsional stiffening, the nonlinear Vlasov equation with restrained warping, and lateral-torsional stability loss with prebuckling deflection.
  - Example `examples/thin_walled/large_twist`.

- **Second-order geometric stiffness, P-delta and linear buckling for the linear beam elements** ([element_library/second_order.md](element_library/second_order.md)):
  - Euler-Bernoulli, Timoshenko, Benscoter, Reddy and Levinson elements build `K_σ` from the energy of a section that is rigid in its own plane, using its current stresses. The section terms `[N, S_y, S_z, W, V_y, V_z, Q_r]` (`ThinWalledSectionAnalysis.geometric_terms`, `SectionAnalysis.geometric_terms`) bring in the shear-centre and tension-centre offsets, the Wagner term and the bimoment for any reference axis.
  - P-delta static solves: `[Static] second_order = p_delta` (`processing/buckling/p_delta.py`).
  - Checked against Euler, Engesser, flexural-torsional (Timoshenko & Gere), lateral-torsional (uniform moment, monosymmetric Wagner, Michell) and beam-column closed forms. Example `examples/thin_walled/channel_buckling`.
  - The previous `K_σ` of the linear EB and Timoshenko elements is replaced; it had no torsional terms and an `N + M/L` factor. The nonlinear elements keep `GeometricStiffnessOperator` until the large-displacement work.
  - The buckling eigensolver no longer symmetrises a nonsymmetric `K` (Levinson).

- **Wall and ply stress recovery for thin-walled sections:**
  - `stress_recovery.py`: full mid-surface strains from the kept beam strains (`N_ss = 0`, free or restrained hoop bending), wall resultants, ply stresses in wall and ply axes, and interlaminar `tau_xn`, `tau_sn` and `sigma_nn` by integrating 3D equilibrium through the thickness. `SaintVenantRecovery` takes section forces.
  - `element_recovery.py`: wall stresses at any point of a solved Euler-Bernoulli, Timoshenko, Benscoter or GBT element.
  - `process_job` writes `secondary_results/wall_stresses/wall_stresses.csv` for linear static jobs with thin-walled sections.
  - Worked examples `examples/thin_walled/box_spar` and `examples/thin_walled/three_cell_blade` with figures.
  - Docs: [element_library/thin_walled_stress_recovery.md](element_library/thin_walled_stress_recovery.md).
- **GBT warping modes, shear lag and wall loads:**
  - The GBT element now uses plane-section beam kinematics with all warping in warping modes: the Saint-Venant warping span (always carried) plus optional shear-lag modes from a new `[shear_modes]` column. A support with the warping amplitudes fixed keeps the section plane, which gives shear lag and the warping-restraint end effect; with them free, tip loads give the Saint-Venant beam.
  - **Breaking (unreleased element):** `LinearGBTBeamElement3D` is now a Timoshenko subclass and its nodal extras are `[b_1 .. b_p, a_1, a_1', ..]` instead of `[psi, a_1, a_1', ..]`. `SectionAnalysis` gains `gbt_sv_modes` and `gbt_shear_modes`; `gbt_matrices(m, p_extra)` uses the new strain order.
  - Wall surface loads from a new optional `wall_load.txt` (`[element_id] [wall_id] [p_x] [p_s] [p_n]`): GBT elements get them on every DOF; Timoshenko and Benscoter elements get the resultant (`ThinWalledSectionAnalysis.wall_load_resultant`).
  - `section_geometry.txt` takes an optional `[Section_Mesh]` block to set the wall subdivision per section.
- **Cross-section modes and the GBT element:**

- **Mesh-wide nodal DOF layout** (`pre_processing/element_library/dof_layout.py`): the nodal stride is 6 plus the largest number of extra DOFs any element row asks for (`[warping] = 1`, or the new optional `element_dictionary["extra_dofs"]`).   - `LinearEulerBernoulliBeamElement3D` and `LinearTimoshenkoBeamElement3D` build `D` from a thin-walled `SectionAnalysis` when one is assigned. Euler-Bernoulli uses the shear-rigid classical 4 × 4; Timoshenko uses the full coupled 6 × 6.

### Fixed

- Static jobs whose elements are all curved no longer abort while saving tertiary results (section forces, principal stresses and the summary skip elements with no Gauss data).
- **Thin-walled section analysis is about 30 times faster on many-wall sections.** Wall elements were assembled as dense products over all warping DOFs, so a 356-wall blade cut took 80 s; they are now scattered from their four DOFs (2.4 s, same results).
- **Static jobs whose elements are all curved** (pretwisted, from `precurvature.txt`) no longer abort while summarising secondary results: those elements have no Gauss-point data, and the projector and summary now skip them.
- **Point moments and line loads on beams** (`pre_processing/element_library/beam_load_interpolation.py`). Loads are now spread with each element's own displacement and rotation fields, so they do work-conjugate virtual work. Before, Euler-Bernoulli elements (linear and nonlinear) dropped nodal `M_y` and `M_z` and gave line loads no end moments, and the Timoshenko, Benscoter, GBT, Reddy and Levinson elements applied `M_y` with the wrong sign. All loads are global and right-handed, like the nodal DOFs. The nonlinear Euler-Bernoulli element also counted a load at a node shared by two elements twice. Checked by end loads in all six directions on every linear beam type, interior and turned-beam moments, and the exact nodal response of Hermite elements under a uniform line load (`tests/test_point_moments.py`).
- **Warping meshes (7 DOF per node):** `Element1DBase.assemble_global_dof_indices` now returns indices in the element's own order `[n1 0..5, n2 0..5, n1 W, n2 W]`. Before, it returned them node-major while every 14-DOF beam builds `K_e` with the two warping DOFs last, so `process_job` mixed the warping DOF of node 1 with `u_x` of node 2. Element-level results were unaffected.
